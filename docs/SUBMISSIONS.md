# Submissions

> **About this file.** This is our submission log as it stood when we stopped (2026-10-08), kept
> verbatim: every upload, its cross-validation figures and its leaderboard score. It was written as
> a working document, so it cites things that are not in this repository: analysis scripts
> (`aNNN`), entries of our internal learnings log (`L0NN`), experiment configs by name, other
> internal documents, and paths on our build machine. The members of the best submission, v62,
> are in `config/experiments/`; its build is in [`REPRODUCE.md`](../REPRODUCE.md); a digest of
> what moved the score is in the [README](../README.md#where-the-score-came-from).

Every file written by `scripts/make_submission.py`, appended automatically. **Fill in the
leaderboard column by hand** when a score comes back — that column is the only record of how our
cross-validation maps onto the real metric, and nothing else in the repo can recover it.

Team `amazing-marshmallow`, bucket `prc-2026-amazing-marshmallow`, five submissions per day.
Teams are ranked on their *best* submission, so a poor score costs nothing but quota.

The table lives at the **end of this file** — new rows are appended there, so the
commentary below stays put as the log grows.

**`**Best.**` in a row means best *at the time it was scored*, so several rows carry it. The
current best is the **lowest** leaderboard number, not the last row — v34 scored worse than v33

## What the scores established, and where it stopped holding

**Steer by `cv.predicted_board()`, not by either column alone.**

An earlier version of this section said to steer by `CV ex-LIRF` because pooled CV looked
anti-correlated with the board (Spearman −0.20). That was **range restriction** — the correlation
was taken over only the four models with a logged per-airport breakdown, all within 9 s of each
other. With `b05`/`b02` restored, pooled CV is +0.657 Spearman / +0.988 Pearson. It predicts the
board fine; it just cannot resolve a few seconds.

The rule it produced is falsified by v1 vs v2, the largest board difference on record:
`b05` beats `b02` by **24.81 s** on the board, and `CV ex-LIRF` scores `b05` **0.66 s worse**.

What separates transfer from non-transfer is where a Rome gain comes from. `b05`'s is a covariate
(`flt_no_match`, 2026 rate verified at 1.424%) and it transferred. `x1_maxbin_2047`'s is model
capacity and it did not — CV Rome 933.9, implied 2026 Rome 1164. Every model submitted lands at
1144–1164 in 2026 while spanning 934–1165 in CV.

Ranking the four deep models only — the restricted set that produced the false reading:

| metric | Pearson | Spearman |
|---|---:|---:|
| CV pooled | −0.460 | **−0.200** |
| CV trimmed | +0.554 | +0.400 |
| **CV ex-LIRF** | **+0.965** | **+1.000** |

Pooled CV is *anti*-correlated with the leaderboard. Excluding LIRF orders all four correctly,
and still does with `x1_maxbin_2047` removed, so one anomaly is not driving it. The magnitudes
line up too: ex-LIRF spans 7.7 s across these models against 9.0 s on the board, where pooled CV
spans 42 s for the same 9 s of real difference.

LIRF is 7.7% of departures and 44–56% of the squared error, and that share is 2025 record
defects. `x1_maxbin_2047` has the **best** LIRF CV of any model (934) and the **worst** ex-LIRF
(304.14): it won cross-validation by memorising Rome's defects, which 2026 does not share.

`rmse_lirf_only` is tracked beside it and never steered by — a change that moves only that
number has not been shown to transfer to anything.

**n = 4.** Spearman +1.000 on four points is suggestive, not established.

| submission | CV pooled | leaderboard | gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| v1 `b05_match_aware_groups` | 607.20 | 604.41 | −2.79 |
| v2 `b02_group_stand_runway` | 629.54 | 629.22 | −0.32 |
| **v3 `x02_lgbm_deep`** | **432.81** | **433.80** | **+0.99** |

Three points spanning 196 s of model quality, with gaps of −2.79, −0.32 and +0.99 — mean −0.71,
and **not all the same sign**, so this is scatter around zero rather than a bias to correct for.
`x07` later landed at +2.54, a fourth point in the same band.

**Then v6 missed by +46.45, and the rule needs a caveat.** `x1_maxbin_2047` had the best CV score
we have ever recorded, 390.59, and scored 437.04 — worse than `x07`, which it had beaten by 37 s
in cross-validation. The separator is visible before submitting, in each model's own
**pooled − trimmed** gap:

| submission | CV pooled | CV trimmed | pooled − trimmed | leaderboard | gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v1 `b05_match_aware_groups` | 607.20 | 434.27 | 172.9 | 604.41 | −2.79 |
| v2 `b02_group_stand_runway` | 629.54 | 441.36 | 188.1 | 629.22 | −0.32 |
| v3 `x02_lgbm_deep` | 432.81 | 306.67 | 126.1 | 433.80 | +0.99 |
| v5 `x07_deep_per_airport` | 427.84 | 303.83 | 124.0 | 430.38 | +2.54 |
| **v6 `x1_maxbin_2047`** | **390.59** | 300.63 | **90.0** | **437.04** | **+46.45** |

Every model that transferred kept 124–188 s of its score above the 12-hour trim. The one that
did not had shrunk that to 90. It had not solved the tail; it had memorised 2025's copy of it,
and 2026 carries its own unrelated defects. So a change that moves the pooled score without
moving the trimmed score is fitting ~34 rows that will not recur.

**Read the trimmed number when deciding what to build.** The pooled number remains the metric and
remains the thing to report, but it is only a leaderboard estimate for a model whose tail gap sits
in the range that has transferred.

**The LIRF contamination is in the withheld labels.** `b05` and `b02` differ only in whether the
Network Manager match flag is used. CV said the flag was worth 22.34 s; the leaderboard says
**24.81 s**. The organisers did not clean the corrupt rows, L012's bet pays, and
[L008](CLAUDE_LEARNINGS.md#l008) is resolved in the affirmative by a route that did not require
probing the ranking process.

**No public/private split is visible.** Both results report `used_pairs: 344841`, the full count
of ranking departures, scored against `prc-2026-testsets/truthing.parquet`.

### Reading a result

```bash
mc cat prc/prc-2026-amazing-marshmallow/amazing-marshmallow_v1.parquet_result.json
```

A result file appears beside the upload within a minute, alongside a `_persist.json`. The
`status` field is `Succeeded` on a valid submission; `score` is the RMSE in seconds.

---

*__Why `CV pooled` and `CV trimmed` are blank for v10 onward.__ Both include LIRF, and a
composition's LIRF half is not a model output — it is three override layers (pop27, the sub-cap
hedge, the one-row `aobt` value) with no 2025 analogue to cross-validate. `a090_composition_cv.py`
computes the honest version and it is not comparable: v28's pooled CV using the Rome **model** is
421.09 against a board of 283.41, because the overrides transform Rome and the CV cannot see them.
Filling those cells would invite exactly the wrong inference, so they stay empty and
**`CV trim ex-LIRF`** carries the number the project actually steers by — it excludes LIRF entirely
and is therefore override-independent. Figures are cross-fold (weights fitted on the other fold);
the in-sample version flatters by ~0.4 s (`a081`, `a085`).

__What is still blank, and why.__ v1 and v2 predate per-airport tracking, so no `by_airport`
breakdown exists to compute the figure from — their own rows say so. v10, v11, v15–v19 and v23 are
ExtraTrees compositions over 16, 18 and 20 members whose **member lists were never versioned**:
`docs/compositions/` was created on 2026-09-27 precisely because `data/rank_preds/members.txt` is
mutable working state, and by then those lists were gone. They are not recoverable, and guessing
them would be worse than the blank. Everything else is filled: v3–v9 exactly from `runs.jsonl`,
v25/v26 from `a062`, v27 from `a085`/`a086`, v28 from `a081`.

__v25's row says 26 members; `a062` printed "shipping member list: 27".__ The 222.31 below is that
27-entry list. Which number the built submission used is not recoverable for the same reason.*
